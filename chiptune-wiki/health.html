Cluster A — Hülsbeck and Rainbow Arts (170 → 155, six pages)#

Six interviews and press visits (1987–2011) read once and spread over the pages each serves a different slice of: 64er 4→0, shades 4→0, rainbow-arts 4→0, arkanoid 1→0, plus two neighbours the plan pointed at, great-giana-sisters 1→0 and chris-huelsbeck 1→0.

  • 64er had the listing and the prize but not the competition. It now has the contest from four sides — Hülsbeck's own "Das war im Februar 1986. Ich habe von dem Wettbewerb gelesen und dachte mir, ich mache da mal mit" (Happy Computer 3/87, which also notes 64'er as "eine Schwesterzeitschrift der Happy-Computer"), the 16-year-old's aim ("determined to get at least noticed … I never expected to win"), and, in 1996, "I had no contact with the 'scene'" beside "Winning the first music competition in a german computer mag" as the proudest moment of his career. A dating conflict is now stated rather than smoothed: Wikipedia cites the announcement to 64'er 6/1986 p. 173, Hülsbeck says February 1986; both stand, and shades points at the page where they stand. The Soundmonitor listing came out of the prize ("Danach habe ich mit der Redaktion vereinbart, daß ich noch ein Programm schreibe") and was reprinted in "64'er Extra Nr. 13".
  • shades gained what made him enter (Synthsample "brachten mich auf die Idee") and the order of events that the wiki had backwards-shaped: the MusicMaster play routine existed before the tune, and only the win made him build an editor around it.
  • rainbow-arts now carries two accounts of the hiring side by side (VGMPF/Remix64: the company phones him; his own March-1987 telling: "In der Zwischenzeit habe ich mich mit Rainbow Arts in Verbindung gesetzt"; 2011: "first contracted, then hired"), the Bad Cat delay that cost him the sample-priority credit, and its 1990s DOS side — Lollypop, commissioned from Brain Bug in Aarhus with Vibrants doing the audio (JCH on AdLib SFX, Drax and Metal on music), PC 1994 / Amiga 1995.
  • arkanoid gets one paragraph it was missing: the game is routinely named as the first with sampled drums, and the claim is disputed — Hülsbeck's 1996 "So Martin Galway got the fame with 'Arkanoid'" against Galway's own refusal to claim the invention. Both sides already live on martin-galway; the game page now says so instead of implying a settled first.

Cluster B — JCH's memoir and the editor lineage (155 → 134, eleven pages)#

charles-deenen 4→0, maniacs-of-noise 3→0, tracker-scene-history 3→0, sid-factory-ii 2→0, hewson 2→0, tim-follin 1→0, turbo-assembler 1→0, cheesecutter 1→0, cybernoid-ii 1→0, musicfile 1→0, jeroen-tel 2→0.

Five sections that had no home anywhere in the wiki:

PAL Ø2 = 985248 Hz, NTSC = 1022727 Hz (defBPM's constants, and they agree with the book's 17734475 / 18), one PAL frame = 19656 cycles — which is exactly why defMON's 1× timer preset is $4CC7, and why it can sit on an exact BPM where frame-locked editors cannot. The sid page already had Telengard as a shipped game that pokes registers from BASIC; the number that belongs to the machine is what it costs — one POKE ≈ 5 200 cycles ≈ a quarter of a PAL frame, for qq=1 to 600: next ≈ 672 000. That is why every emulator guide in the wiki opens with a key-mapping table, and why FlexiArcade sells a 3D-printed defMON keyboard overlay. The mapping tables themselves stay on vice.

Why so many, and what was actually missing#

The page was built from the datasheet, the Programmer's Reference Guide, the Yannes interview and Alstrup's measurements — so it was strong on what the silicon does and almost silent on what forty years of players did to it. The 22 unlanded summaries are mostly editors (defMON's four wiki pages and its megathread, CheeseCutter, Soundmonitor) and reverse-engineered drivers (Galway's Arkanoid, Matt Gray's Serpent Demo, the Tiny Sid 256-byte entries), plus four interviews. Deciding what was chip and what was tool was most of the work; recipes went out as pointers, register-level behaviour stayed.

Five things that had no home anywhere in the wiki:

  • A section for $D418. The register does two unrelated jobs — volume in the low nybble, filter mode in the high — so every program that touches one has to preserve the other, and three sources thirty years apart show the same defensive idiom: Soundmonitor's volume byte "almost identical to SID register 24, but without the filter nibble", Galway's lda #$0F / ora TEMP / sta $D418, defMON's two self-modified operands ($10B1, "Make sure that the upper nibble is always zero"). The volume-register fourth voice then gets its three generations in one place: 1986 Galway, who explicitly disclaims the invention ("Never would I claim to have invented that technique, I just got it published first") and whose Arkanoid percussion turns out to contain no PCM data at all — six routines adding a constant to a 4-bit accumulator through a halving delay table; 1987 Matt Gray's fourth pattern channel streaming nibbles from a CIA-2 NMI while the raster IRQ plays the SID voices; 1991 JCH hacking digis into his own editor and making exactly four tunes with them.
  • Two SIDs, or three. The page treated the chip as singular. Now: the chip is mono, so stereo means a second chip; defMON's selectable bases $D4xx/$D5xx/$DExx/$DFxx (and the warning that a wrong one corrupts RAM); the cost in software — a second full copy of the player, no packing for 2SID tunes, and frantic's "about 95% of all bugs I have fixed in defMON over the years were related to that"; and hermit writing a 3SID tracker on a real C64 in four months of 2008, four years before sid-wizard, because "DMC 4.3 and Prophet64" were the only 2SID editors he could find.
  • Software can ask which chip it is on. defMON's init detects 6581 vs 8580 (disabling interrupts while it does, which is an integration trap) and then acts on the answer, hard-capping the filter cutoff at $0200 on an 8580 and $0000 on a 6581 "to make the chips behave more alike". TLR's sid-detect2.prg finds a second chip at an unknown address.
  • The filter clicks when you switch it — less on the 8580. soundmonitor 1986 gives a sound a FF "touch no filter register" value "which avoids the SID's click when filters switch"; freedom 2010 gives the positive form — on the 8580 the click is faint enough that "it is possible to change filter type on an instrument while a note is being played". Twenty-four years, two sources, one artefact, and a technique that only exists on the newer chip.
  • The MIDIbox SID. The page had the chip driven from a PC (Catweasel) but not the chip in a MIDI synthesizer. It also supplies the wiki's definition of "wavetable" in the C64 sense, and the envelope bug with a number on it: "The gate is delayed by up to ca. 30mS!"

Findings worth keeping#

  • The noise-reset trick has a discoverer and a date. The page already documented that the test bit restarts the noise shift register (Alstrup's measurements, 4mat's $f9 then $81 recipe). JCH's memoir attributes it: johannes-bjerregaard, visiting him around 1988, "discovered how to use a test bit in the SID chip to reset the random numbers used internally by the noise waveform", making noise "more predictable … slightly more metallic" — and JCH's scanned papers include a draft magazine article about using the test bit when composing. frantic states the mechanism cleanly for tracker users: it "resets the oscillator that produces the waveform, and that is completely independent of the volume envelope generation".
  • A contradiction narrowed by released music. The page carried "the OSC3/ENV3 readback was never used" (Furnace; Yannes's own "probably never used") against the PRG's examples and Alstrup's measurements. Three 2005 tiny-sid-compo entries settle it as released music: $D41B picks note positions (lda $d41b / and #7 / sta pos) and self-modifies waveforms, and aleksi-eeben derives the filter cutoff from $D41C (lda $D41C / lsr / adc #$20) — the datasheet's own "WAH WAH" suggestion, in a 256-byte tune twenty-three years later. Read "never used" as "rarely, and then mostly where code size mattered more than a voice".
  • Two tools agree on the silent combined waveforms. CheeseCutter's cheat sheet — "Waveforms $3, $6 and $7 are audible only on new SID (8580)" — is exactly Chordian's 31/61/71 audibility table, reached from a different editor in a different decade.
  • Microtuning is free on the SID and not on the Game Boy, and the reason is one line of the datasheet: 16 bits of frequency divider. little-scale's 2009 compilation call names the SID among chips with "a frequency resolution that is fine enough for the composition of microtonal music"; lsdj needs its ROM note table patched, a SID editor needs nothing.
  • The cutoff's low three bits can simply go unwritten — anarkiwi's register dump shows defMON never touches $D415, "so the rightmost two ACID digits of a set value do nothing".
  • A 1983 game that pokes the SID from BASIC. Telengard zeroes all 25 registers, computes a 60-entry note table by one multiplication per semitone (r=61176/64814), and times notes by how long the interpreter takes — the Programmer's Reference Guide style as shipped software, and the reason its rip had to become an RSID.
  • Emulation, four verdicts, four different missing pieces: hermit 2008 (the analogue filter, and "the note-frequency dependent samplerate of the SID"), goto80 2002 ("Don't imitate, innovate — fuck emulators"), frantic 2020 ("It's like a fractal maze that drives you more mad the closer you try to get to it", and defMON is native so you work on the real chip), and electro·pizza 2019 arriving from the other side — VICE on a Pocket CHIP is for learning defMON, "especially useful in experimenting with and constructing instruments and effects in the SID tab", not for performing.
