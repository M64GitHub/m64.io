 must cherish these times and capture them in our newsletter to show our

 The readers of this newsletter span the globe, so think globally. The concept
 of this newsletter is to focus on the musicians on IRC and in the demo scene,
 so take that into consideration. People always like a good laugh, so use
 humor where appropriate. Write to inform, not to misinform. Provide the facts

 -[Plagiarism]----------------------------------------------------------------
 
 After reading Pinion's article in TraxWeekly 12 about plagiarism I got
 inspired to write this. I think that the only way of preventing cowards from
 stealing songs is by discouraging them in public by means of writing stuff
 like this.
 
 This message is meant to straighten up some things in the PC music scene.
 Even in a small country like The Netherlands, there's people around who just
 rip your name out of a song and put their name in it. I found this out a
 month ago, with help from my friends at Sound Distribution Channel BBS.
 
 'Club Bizarre', a song released for Epinicion, had been abused by Sound Board
 Connection's sysop Hennes Eliveld. Mr. Lazy ripped out the composer's name in
 the song description and put in some crap name like 'The Inspirators Germany'
 (Gosh, speaking of inspiration...who the hell are these guys anyway?)
 
 Also, he changed the song name completely.....Hiawatha??!! (This name makes
 me want to puke). When I tracked the song, it surely didn't have that name
 on it.
 
 One more thing to be aware of is the fact that Sound Board Connection is a 
 music network on a commercial base. In fact, they're making money outta songs
 you originally tracked... and you don't even get any credit for it! It's like
 sneaking around somebody's back.
 
 There has always been one major rule in the PC-music composing scene: RESPECT
 THE SONGS OF OTHER COMPOSERS! Don't mess with them! If you can't compose your
 own piece of music or you're too lazy for it, then just don't. Even worse,
 making money out of songs which have been stolen makes me sick! Hopefully, it
 won't happen again in the future.
 
 Bert [Epinicion]

                     A BRIEF BUT OFFICIAL HISTORY OF OTM
 
                             Compiled by Voltaire
 
 OTM is a demo group which is based in Phoenix Arizona, USA. Currently we have
 eight members: Zilym Limms, Hurricane, Voltaire, Phred, Stalker, Tek,
 Supreme, and Nameshift.
 
 OTM began its life in December of 1993 as a group known as Gentilezza
 (jen-tuhl-EHT-sah), which was founded by Zilym Limms (a music coder) and
 Stalker (a musician). Soon after founding Gentilezza, Stalker and Zilym
 invited one of Stalker's longtime friends, Supreme, to join as an artist.
 Supreme generously made his BBS, The Devil's Gate, into Gentilezza's World
 Headquarters. TDG proved instrumental in bringing another talented musician
 to Gentilezza as Tek, a user of TDG, offered some of his work as a tryout to
 get into the group. Zilym, Stalker, and Supreme were very impressed with
 Tek's work and immediately offered him a place as a musician.
 
 Several months later, Zilym offered Hurricane a position as a graphics coder.
 Zilym and Hurri had been working together for some time on projects involving
 digital sound and music, which eventually evolved into Bells, Whistles, and
 Sound Boards (BWSB), Zilym's excellent sound system. Like Tek, Hurri also
 made a tryout production in order to officially be voted in as a member of
 the group. He was unanimously accepted, and several months later invited a
 friend of his, Voltaire, to join Gentilezza as another graphics coder.
 Following the tradition set forth by Tek and Hurri, Volt also made a tryout
 demo and was unanimously accepted into the group.
 
 At this point (June 1994) Gentilezza began to take steps which would allow it
 to become a well respected group. Zilym was close to finishing his sound and
 music engine, Volt began work on a 3d engine, Stalker and Tek were honing
 their skills as musicians, and Supreme was becoming an accomplished artist.
 This is a stage at which many groups remain for quite some time; fortunately
 the members of Gentilezza became very good friends and encouraged each other
 to keep working and making progress.
 
 During the summer of 1994, it was very difficult to stay focused as a group.
 Zilym was gone for an extended vacation, Supreme was a counsellor in training
 at a summer camp, Volt went to Livermore California to learn how to program
 supercomputers at a government laboratory. Despite a lack of group cohesion
 during this time, much was accomplished. Zilym continued to polish his sound
 code, Volt made his 3d engine better and faster, Stalker and Tek became more
 adept at tracking.
 
 Most notably however, Gentilezza emerged from the summer of 1994 with a new
 name: OverThrowMachine, or OTM. This change was a bit of a coup which
 occurred when Zilym (who had christened the group Gentilezza) was out of town
 and unable to defend himself.
 
 Soon after OTM changed its name, more changes were in store. The group
 realized that Supreme would have to carry a large workload being the only
 artist, so Volt suggested that OTM consider a tryout from Nameshift, a
 talented artist formerly belonging to another Arizona based group named mAx.
 OTM found NS to be a very talented artist, and was happy to accept him as
 part of the team.
 
 A short time after NS joined, Hurri and Volt decided that another talented
 coder would help the group tremendously, and suggested that Phred join OTM.
 Zilym and Volt had a long running correspondence with Phred regarding all
 types of coding from music to vectors, and they were extremely confident in
 his abilities. Phred's work impressed everyone in OTM so much that he was
 unanimously voted in as well.
 
 At this point, the winter of 1994, OTM was comprised of its present members.
 It was time to start working on a production, but no one really wanted to
 lead the charge. Fortunately, Hurri caught the vision of a truly original
 production, a demo done totally in character based textmode which
 incorporated "traditional" demo effects.  Hurri began work on his "Textro",
 and solicited Phred to help him out, mainly because Phred was the only other
 member of OTM who didn't laugh at the idea of a textmode demo. Soon, Hurri
 began distributing previews of his work, and the rest of OTM caught the
 textmode bug.
 
 Hurri proved to be a good leader, delegating responsibilities to members of
 the group in such a way that everyone's talents were used. The result was
 Textro, an interesting and original production which received resoundingly
 positive reviews.
 
 The history of OTM, although brief, has been fun to experience. OTM
 represents a new breed of demo group which places a greater emphasis on
 production design rather than bleeding edge code and effects. This new twist
 results in productions which are fun to make, fun to experience, and which
 utilize the potential of each member of the group to its fullest extent.
 While OTM has been somewhat obscure in the past, rest assured that we will
 become well known in the future, a future which is guaranteed to be bright.

 Productions, let me offer a brief description: The idea behind pHluid is
 to offer a high quality music production every month. I wanted to do
 something that everyone on the music scene could look forward to. I find
 it really difficult to wade through all of the single files on ftp sites. 
 Every week Hornet seems to get a new barrage of single modules and I just
 don't have the time to download all of them. Hence, I started the pHluid 
 project to put all the files in, more or less, one place.
 
 The web page is under construction and should be presentable within the 
 next few weeks. It will include member lists, file links, links to other 
 relevant sites, and of course the funky fresh graphics. For right now, you
 can ftp the disk from it at: http://acid.extern.ucsd.edu.  This is the ACiD
 home page, but it has links to the pHluid page.
 
 I am happy to report that pHluid has two new members for this month. Future
 Assassin and Lord Pegasus have joined on to do music for the next disk. I
 have heard Lord Pegasus's song for the July disk entitled "Destination Venus"
 and it is really good.
 
 If you haven't already downloaded yourself a copy of the first pHulid music
 disk, then you can get it from ftp.cdrom.com: demos/music/disks/, on the
 ACiD Web page at: http://acid.extern.ucsd.edu, or on #acid (/msg monkeydcc).
 
 pHluid is still open for applicants, so if you're interested in joining, send
 your iquiries to: pinion@acid.extern.ucsd.edu. Attach files using either MIME
 (pine) or just UUencode them and attach them to the text body.

 of my email forwarded, so there should be no problem getting a hold of me.
 
 Look for the next disk early in July.

 monkeydcc), and at the ACiD Home Page (http://acid.extern.ucsd.edu)
