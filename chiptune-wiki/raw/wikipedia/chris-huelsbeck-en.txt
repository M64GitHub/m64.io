Christopher Hülsbeck (born 2 March 1968),[1] professionally known as Chris Huelsbeck, is a German video game music composer who has worked in the industry since the 1980s.[2] His early work includes music for games such as The Great Giana Sisters and the Turrican series.[2] He later worked on the music for Factor 5 titles such as Star Wars: Rogue Squadron and its sequels.[2]

## Career
[edit]

Huelsbeck's professional career began in 1986, when his composition Shades won a music competition organized by the German computer magazine 64'er.[3][4] This led to a position at Rainbow Arts,[4] where he composed music for games including The Great Giana Sisters.[5]

Huelsbeck also developed music tools for home computers. In 1986, he published SoundMonitor for the Commodore 64[6] and later co-created the TFMX ("The Final Musicsystem eXtended") sound editor with programmer Peter Thierolf.[7]

During the early 1990s, Huelsbeck composed music for the Turrican series and co-founded the game developer Kaiko, whose releases included Gem'X and Apidya.[7] In 1998, he moved to the United States to join Factor 5, where he worked on the Star Wars: Rogue Squadron series.[2]

Huelsbeck continued composing in later years, including Giana Sisters: Twisted Dreams (2012), Tiny Thor (2023) and X-Out: Resurfaced (2025).[8][9][10]

### Orchestra performances
[edit]

Huelsbeck's music was performed in the Game Concerts series in Leipzig between 2003 and 2007, including works from Apidya, Turrican II, Turrican 3 and The Great Giana Sisters.[11]

On 23 August 2008, Symphonic Shades was presented in Cologne as a concert devoted entirely to Huelsbeck's music, performed by the WDR Radio Orchestra and the FILMharmonic Choir Prague.[12] It was the first video game music concert to be broadcast live on the radio.[13] In 2009, selections from Symphonic Shades were performed by the Royal Stockholm Philharmonic Orchestra as part of Sinfonia Drammatica, alongside music by Yoko Shimomura.[14]
